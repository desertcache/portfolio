01 Featured program

A partner sunset its API. The program I ran kept 94% of the revenue at risk.

A major POS partner deprecated its v1 API with thousands of active stores still on it. This is the whole forward deployed loop in one program, start to finish in eight months: size the loss, fund the fix, build the measurement, drive the adoption.

  1. Problem

    Thousands of active stores on a dying API, and significant monthly merchant revenue exposed if they didn't move in time. Organic outreach was moving under 1% of them a week, nowhere near the timeline.

  2. Bet

    Co-authored the funding request: sized the risk, modeled four investment options, and recommended white-glove vendor support for the highest-revenue stores. The model projected most of the revenue saved for a fraction of the cost of covering every store.

  3. Build

    Designed the operating model end to end: CRM-routed queues, a 3-day cadence, 200–300 touches a day, training, and escalation paths. Then built the dashboard that ran the program, with the full funnel, migration progress, and revenue at every stage in one view.

  4. Result

Context

Support operations ran 100+ queues across multiple vendors on legacy SaaS, with the numbers spread across several data sources.

What I built

A production workforce platform that pulls those sources into one operating surface: 18 dashboard pages covering queue health, SLA, and Erlang C staffing on live Snowflake data. Micro-frontend architecture, plus a typed component library published to the internal registry.

The hard part

The upstream systems didn't agree on names: only about 5% of records matched exactly. Fuzzy matching lifted that to 95%+.

Outcome

An operations lead outside the team independently validated the numbers within 0.9% of the legacy system. The platform has shipped on a weekly release cadence through 2026.

Context

I built a production AI agent for merchant support, and it had an adoption problem. The feedback from support agents: “wall of text, then abandon.”

What I built

Rebuilt the prompt architecture around an enforced six-section answer: What This Means, Diagnostic Checklist, Action Matrix, Outreach Script, Still Stuck?, and CRM Reminder. Prose became something an agent can scan and act on.

Outcome

Average answers shrank from ~1,500 to ~300 words while holding accuracy. 76 monthly active users, 6,846 queries served, top adoption among the internal AI agents, and two more agents now reuse the template.

Context

Merchant-to-account mapping lived in spreadsheets, with turnaround measured in days: one to seven of them.

What I built

A dual-stack product: React frontend, a Fastify backend-for-frontend, and Snowflake underneath. I'm the declared package owner, and it's now embedded in the broader workforce platform.

The hard part

The bugs a team loses weeks to, fixed at the driver level: a snowflake-sdk browser-auth race that looped and opened multiple tabs (solved with promise-based connection locking), a typed callback that silently terminated the warehouse connection, and a silent 1,000-row cap on the AI query service (solved with split queries and a truncation banner).

Outcome

Hundreds of thousands of records flowing through production across hundreds of programs, with 26 of 35 phase-1 features live.

Context

Troubleshooting a single merchant issue meant juggling five or more internal tabs.

What I built

A single-page app embedded in the internal admin portal: 22 components, dual lookup by store ID or case ID, four issue-specific diagnostic checklists, and an AI assistant wired in through the enterprise search platform.

The hard part

Telemetry that fails quietly. I documented three subtle behaviors as a team reference: dual entity registration, missing user-ID propagation, and silent SDK failures. Later I extracted the app into a standalone micro-frontend, which survived a same-day platform pivot.

Outcome

Build to pilot in about four weeks, 19 instrumented event types, and one surface addressable to thousands of support agents.

  • React 18

  • GraphQL

Context

A personal setup for my own engineering work, not a DoorDash system.

What I built

Six agents covering dispatch, code review, repo stewardship, daily standup prep, memory audit, and orchestration. A local-only background daemon coordinates spawn timing, a classifier triages incoming work, and a commander spawns agents into terminal tabs.
