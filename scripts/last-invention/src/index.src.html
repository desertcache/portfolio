Act two

The Loop

What happens when AI starts building AI.

Act two is about why this does not simply continue. It accelerates, and the reason is a loop.
“The first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make.”
I. J. Good · 1965 Codebreaker beside Alan Turing at Bletchley Park. Later an adviser to Stanley Kubrick on 2001: A Space Odyssey.

Good named the idea an intelligence explosion: a machine smart enough to design a better machine, which designs a better one still.

I. J. Good wrote this in 1965. He worked with Turing on breaking Enigma, and later advised Kubrick on HAL. His point: a machine that can improve machines starts a chain reaction. He called it an intelligence explosion. Remember the quote; it has a second half.

Each era grew 150 to 250 times faster than the one before. One more jump and the world economy doubles in weeks. Even 30% growth a year, a scenario economists who model AI take seriously, doubles it every 2.6 years.

Doubling times of the world economy: R. Hanson, 2000. Explosive growth: T. Davidson, Open Philanthropy, 2021. GDP: Maddison Project, approximate.

Robin Hanson fit the whole history of the world economy to a few growth modes. Foragers doubled every 224,000 years, farmers every 909, industry every 6.3. Click: each jump was 150 to 250 times faster. One more jump means doubling in weeks. Even the explosive-growth case economists study, 30 percent a year, is unlike anything in history.

The Fork

Act four. Good's quote had a second half, and it is the part that matters most. Everything depends on keeping control.

What is at stake

Same technology. Two futures.

Flourishing

Disease, poverty and drudgery end. Abundance and exploration. Lives longer and richer than we can picture.

The work that decides which one
AlignmentMake sure it wants what we want.
ControlKeep the power to check it and correct it.
GovernanceMake sure no single group holds all the keys.

Catastrophe

Systems we cannot correct. Weapons anyone can build. Power concentrated in a few hands.

Same technology, two outcomes. The difference comes down to three pieces of work: alignment, control and governance. These get decided in the next few years, and most people do not know the decisions are being made.
