Hill Money Watch — September 23, 2026

Nancy Pelosi's household opened a $3M–12M position in Bloom Energy and added to Intel, the first move on either side of the open Intel divergence since June. Cleo Fields's missing August buy turned up after all, April McClain Delaney sold 23 positions of $100K or more in one day, and the chair of the House Ethics Committee disclosed three trades about eight months after they were made.

September 23, 2026

Catch-up edition. This one covers filings published from August 17 through September 23, five and a half weeks since the August 16 edition, so it runs long. It is ordered by what a reader would act on, not by date. Two corrections to earlier editions are under Data Notes.

The Headlines

Nancy Pelosi's household bought into Bloom Energy and added to Intel. A filing signed August 21 shows 15,000 Bloom shares and 200 long-dated Bloom calls, $3M–12M by the disclosed bands, plus 10,000 Intel shares and 50 more Intel calls. The Intel add is the first move on either side of the open Pelosi–Evans Intel divergence since June.

Cleo Fields's August buy, whose absence the last edition flagged, turned up: Apple on August 13, filed September 2. Outside the roster, Rep. April McClain Delaney (D-MD) sold 23 positions of $100K or more in a single day, and Tier-2 name Michael Guest, who chairs the House Ethics Committee, disclosed three family-trust sales about eight months after they were made.

Pelosi's pattern is large in-the-money calls bought through her husband's account, and the Bloom calls fit it: Decrypt puts the share price at roughly $167–185 on the purchase days, far above the $100 strike. What is new is that both names got shares alongside the options, where May's filing was options only. By the bands, Bloom is $3M–12M of the filing's $4.25M–14.5M total.

The timing, as reported: Bloom posted record second-quarter revenue after the close on July 28, the day of the second lot, and the shares were up 26.5% by July 30 (Decrypt). The filing was signed August 21, and the stock rose in premarket trading on Tuesday, August 25 as it drew coverage. S&P Dow Jones Indices then announced Bloom's addition to the S&P 500 on September 4, effective September 21. A reader acting on the disclosure was buying after the earnings move and before the index news.

One structural point for anyone following these positions: every option in the household's 2026 filings expires after Pelosi leaves office in January. That covers May's Intel and Uber calls (March 2027) and these (June 2027). Members stop filing transaction reports when they leave, so however these positions are closed may never show up on this tape.

Any filing from a Tier-2 name is news, and this one comes from the chair of the committee that administers late-filing fines. The three sales were made by a family trust owned by Guest's wife and disclosed in a message to the House Clerk on Friday, August 14. That is about eight months after the trades and more than six months past the 45-day window. NOTUS, which broke it on August 17, reported it as his second STOCK Act violation, after a 2021 filing more than eight months late on ExxonMobil and BP sales by the same trust.

Guest wrote that "a compliance firm missed them, discovering the oversight while preparing my annual House disclosure," and his chief of staff said he "does not have any decision-making role" in the trust. Per NOTUS, he intends to pay any fine and sees "no need to recuse." As a trade there is nothing here: three small lots, sold at a loss by Guest's account. Its relevance is to the policy debate, not to anyone's portfolio.

Dwight Evans, who sold Intel in May and June as part of his exit run, has filed nothing since June 10. No new clusters among tracked names this window. The nearest thing is Apple, with Fields buying while Sessions (and, in the August 16 edition, Thanedar) sold, but Fields is the only tracked name in it.

Policy Watch

H.R. 7008, the Stop Insider Trading Act, passed the House 232–198 on July 22. Earlier editions misnumbered it; see Data Notes. Per sponsor Rep. Bryan Steil's office, it bars members, spouses and dependent children from buying individual stocks, lets them keep what they already hold, requires public notice 7 to 14 days before any sale, and sets penalties at the greater of $2,000 or 10% of the investment, plus forfeiture of profits. It also carries voter-ID language from the SAVE America Act, which Democrats have called a poison pill for the Senate (Gray DC).

No Senate floor action surfaced through September 23. Majority Leader John Thune told CNN in July he doubted leadership would bring the bill up, and Sen. Josh Hawley's separate HONEST Act has largely stalled (Gray DC). If the House bill ever becomes law, this tape changes character: sales would be announced a week or two before they happen, which would be the first time this data led a trade instead of trailing it.

Data Notes

Correction: the bill number. The August 15 and 16 editions called the House-passed bill H.R. 1908. It is H.R. 7008, the Stop Insider Trading Act, per Rep. Steil's July 23 release and contemporaneous coverage. The vote and the date were right. Both posts now carry a correction, which also fixes what they said enactment would mean: advance notice of sales, not forced sales.

Correction: Fields's "absence." The August 16 edition said Cleo Fields was five weeks past his buying cadence and that the absence was becoming the signal. He had bought Apple on August 13, three days before that edition, and filed it September 2. The gap was disclosure lag. An absence in this data only means something once the 45-day window on it has closed.

Pelosi's filing date. The PTR is signed August 21, a Friday; several trackers and outlets give August 24, when coverage began. This page uses the signature date. Decrypt put all 200 Bloom calls on the July 28 lot; the filing splits them 100 and 100.

Tracker disagreements. Delaney's filing date is September 9 on congressstock.com and in MarketBeat's alerts; AltIndex's "+16 days" implies September 10. The two trackers also disagree on the Live Nation lot ($100K–250K vs. $250K–500K), which is why the Delaney total is a wide range. "Bank of Nova Scotia" on the Biggs and Khanna filings is a Scotia-issued note, not BNS shares: Biggs's filing describes corporate bonds and notes, Khanna's an S&P 500-linked note. AltIndex nonetheless tags Khanna's line as a BNS share purchase of $50K–100K filed +11d, against congressstock's $100K–250K filed September 4. Separately, CapitolExposed's September 7–14 roundup lists a Rep. John Rose (R-TN) fund sale of up to $1M, traded June 3, 2025, as that week's only $100K+ trade. Per Investing.com, that trade was reported at the time and later corrected to name the right fund, so it is left out here.

Coverage gaps. OGE refused every connection this pass, so Trump's September 278-T is located but unread. CapitolExposed's roundups for the late-August weeks returned no content. Ro Khanna's July TD filing date is still disputed three ways, with no primary filing found.

Source change. NOTUS relaunched as The Washington Sun on September 9, and its older links now redirect there. This page cites stories under the name they ran under.

Standing caveats: dollar figures are disclosed ranges, never exact amounts. Totals here add up the ends of the bands, so they are ranges too. Spouse and dependent transactions attribute to the member. Trackers routinely disagree on lot counts and filing dates.

On late filings. Late disclosures are common and frequently clerical: a missed form, a spouse's account, a filing agent's backlog. This page reports the gap between trade and disclosure dates exactly as filed, and attributes any characterization of a filing as a violation to the outlet that made it. Whether a given filing actually breaks the law is for the House Ethics Committee to determine, not this page.

The lag caveat, every edition. Disclosures lag trades by up to 45 days. Ranges are as filed. This edition's lead item became public four weeks after the trades and after the stock's earnings move. Everything here is a disclosure of a trade that has already happened at a price that has already moved. This is disclosure reporting, not investment advice, and it is not a real-time feed of anyone's positions.
