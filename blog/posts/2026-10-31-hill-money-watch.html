Hill Money Watch: October 31, 2026

Halloween edition. Cleo Fields is back buying chips: three Nvidia lots and one TSMC lot, filed October 22. Two catch-ups the last editions missed: Pelosi's October 2 report, $500K–1M into a San Francisco office-tower LLC, and Trump's 517-trade August report, led by a $5M–25M Meta buy. Off the roster, Sen. Dave McCormick's household put $600K–1.25M back into Goldman Sachs.

October 31, 2026

Trick or treat. Happy Halloween from the disclosure desk. The treats are real filings: Fields back in chips, the McCormicks' Goldman homecoming, and Trump's accounts loading up on Meta. The tricks were played on this page. Two reports slipped past the trackers it leans on, so Pelosi's October 2 filing and Trump's October 8 report arrive here a few weeks late, like trick-or-treaters who missed the first lap of the block.

Window. New filings published October 12 through October 31, the 19 days since the October 12 edition. The newest filings any tracker shows are from Thursday, October 29; anything posted October 30 or 31 had not reached them by this run and rolls to the next edition. Two items are catch-ups that earlier editions missed: Pelosi's October 2 report and Trump's August report, posted October 8.

The Headlines

Cleo Fields is buying chips again. The buy-only accumulator filed on October 22: three Nvidia lots on October 1 and one Taiwan Semiconductor lot on October 9, $1K–15K each, in his Morgan Stanley E*TRADE IRA (+21d and +13d). It is his first Nvidia since the June 26 triple and his first TSMC since July 10. This confirms his pattern rather than changing it.

Pelosi filed on October 2, and the last two editions missed it. PTR #20035553 has one line: her husband's account put $500K–1M into REOF XXX, LLC on September 8, an LLC "acquiring and managing a commercial office property at 225 Bush Street in San Francisco" (+24d). It is private, so there is nothing to trade, and it closes none of the Bloom, Intel or Uber calls that expire after she leaves office.

Trump's August report was posted on October 8, before the last edition, which said it was not up yet. It lists 517 transactions, which CNBC put at $74.3M–273.3M. The largest is a Meta purchase of $5M–25M on August 21. The same day brought $1M–5M buys of AT&T, ConocoPhillips, Abbott, Netflix and Chevron and $1M–5M sales of AMD and Church & Dwight. The White House says outside advisers run these accounts on model portfolios benchmarked to market indexes: read them as portfolio moves, not stock picks.

Off the roster, Sen. Dave McCormick's household went back into Goldman Sachs: three spouse purchases between October 7 and 20, $600K–1.25M in total, filed October 29. His wife, Dina Powell McCormick, spent 16 years at Goldman, and Benzinga reported in February that her January 23 sale emptied the position. Also new: Lisa McClain (Tier 2) filed nine trades on October 12, and Sen. Alan Armstrong, the former Williams CEO, sold $250K–500K of Williams.

Cross-Signals

Microsoft: a cluster by the rule, thin by the dollars. Fields bought two lots on September 10 (reported October 3), and McClain's household bought on September 30, 20 days later. That puts two tracked members on the same side inside 30 days, which this page counts as a cluster. Trump's accounts add a $1M–5M buy on August 10. Two cautions apply. Both members' lots are $1K–15K. And Trump's accounts sold $5M–25M of Microsoft on July 20 and partly bought it back three days later, so their net direction over the summer is unclear. Khanna's dependent also bought on September 4, from accounts that trade both sides.

Netflix: the same shape, weaker. Trump's accounts bought $1M–5M on August 21, and Fields bought on September 14, 24 days later. Khanna's household was on both sides in September. One managed portfolio plus one small directional buyer meets the rule, but only just.

Nvidia: no signal. Fields bought three lots on October 1. Trump's accounts traded it both ways in August ($500K–1M bought August 17, $500K–1M sold August 21).

Intel: unchanged. Pelosi's October 2 filing touched none of her Intel shares or calls, and Evans has filed nothing in the 143 days since his June 10 sale.

Seagate, for context only: McClain's household sold on September 30, and Kevin Hern, who is off the roster, sold on October 23.

Data Notes

Corrections to the October 3 and October 12 editions. Both said Pelosi had filed nothing new, but her PTR #20035553, signed October 2, fell inside the October 3 edition's window. The October 12 edition also said Trump's August report had not been posted, though OGE published it on October 8. On late filings. Late disclosures are common and frequently clerical: a missed form, a spouse's account, a filing agent's backlog. This page reports the gap between trade and disclosure dates exactly as filed, and attributes any characterization of a filing as a violation to the outlet that made it. Whether a given filing actually breaks the law is for the Ethics Committee to determine, not this page.

The lag caveat, every edition. Disclosures lag trades by up to 45 days. Ranges are as filed. Everything here is a disclosure of a trade that has already happened at a price that has already moved. This is disclosure reporting, not investment advice, and it is not a real-time feed of anyone's positions.
