Hill Money Watch — August 16, 2026

August 16, 2026

Three House members disclosed late trades on the same day, led by a $100K–250K Apple sale Shri Thanedar sat on for seven months. The Tier-1 roster is quiet across the board, Cleo Fields is now five weeks past his own buying cadence, and Ro Khanna's TD filing date has picked up a third conflicting value.

Window. Filings surfaced since Friday, August 14. The House Clerk does not publish over the weekend, so the roster tape itself is genuinely empty — but a batch of late disclosures went up Thursday and was reported Friday, after the August 15 edition had already gone out. Those are new to this page.

The Headlines

Three House members disclosed late trades on the same day, and one of them clears the reporting bar on size alone. Rep. Shri Thanedar (D-MI) sold $100,001–$250,000 of Apple in January and did not disclose it until Thursday — roughly seven months past the STOCK Act's 45-day deadline. That is the only item in this batch big enough to matter as a position; the rest matter as a pattern.

Alongside him, Rep. Derek Tran (D-CA) was about a year late disclosing a Litecoin sale, and Rep. Tracey Mann (R-KS) was nearly two years late on ten tech-heavy trades made by his wife. Counting the Rulli and Johnson batches from the August 15 edition, that is five members caught filing late inside about a week — while a stock-trading ban sits in the Senate.

The names actually worth trading are silent. No Tier-1 member has filed anything new, and the roster's own baselines are unchanged in every case.

Policy Watch

H.R. 1908, the congressional stock-trading ban, passed the House 232–198 on July 22, 2026 and remains pending in the Senate. No Senate action surfaced this period; the chamber is in August recess.

The relevant development is not legislative, it is evidentiary. Five members in roughly a week — Rulli, Johnson, Thanedar, Tran, Mann — have been caught filing past a 45-day deadline whose maximum penalty starts at $200. If the ban is enacted, the signal in this dataset shifts from buys to forced-sale notices, which is a very different tape to read and worth being positioned for beforehand.
