Hill Money Watch: October 3, 2026

Cleo Fields filed five new tech buys on October 1, Trump's accounts disclosed 1,156 July trades including $5M–25M sales of Microsoft and Amazon, and the Senate killed the House stock-trading ban 53–47 on September 30.

October 3, 2026

Window. New filings published September 23 through October 3, the ten days since the September 23 catch-up edition. Today is a Saturday, so the newest filings are Thursday's and Friday's.

The Headlines

Cleo Fields filed five new buys on October 1, all $1K–15K lots: Apple (Sep 8), Microsoft (Sep 10, two lots), Alphabet and Netflix (both Sep 14). The September 23 edition said a September buy would likely surface by mid-October; it came two weeks early, on his usual 17 to 23 day lag.

The Trump filing the last edition could only locate is now readable, and it is not a bond filing. The report signed September 8 lists 1,156 trades dated July 1–31 (716 sales, 440 purchases), with the largest lots being $5M–25M sales of both Microsoft and Amazon on July 20. The disclosed ranges add up to roughly $79M–270M. The White House says the accounts are run by third-party managers with no input from the family.

On Wednesday, the Senate voted 53–47 against cloture on H.R. 7008, the Stop Insider Trading Act, short of the 60 needed. Nothing else on the roster moved: Pelosi, Evans, Rick Scott and McClain have filed nothing new.

The pattern is rebalancing at industrial volume: an average of 77 trades per active day and more than 300 on July 29 alone, per The Washington Sun. The same coverage lists energy (ExxonMobil, Chevron, Gulfport), defense (Palantir, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, GE Aerospace) and a $500K Axon purchase among the July lines. Dollar totals differ by outlet: roughly $79M–270M at CNBC and Yahoo, $70M–230M at The Washington Sun. Counts differ too: 1,156 nearly everywhere, 1,152 in one headline.

The White House's explanation, from spokesperson Davis Ingle as quoted by The Washington Sun, is that "all holdings are maintained in discretionary accounts" and that neither Trump nor his family can "direct, influence, or provide input." Yahoo reports a related statement that the portfolio is made up of "computer-based model portfolios that automatically replicate recognized indexes, such as the Schwab 1000." That framing fits the volume: this is not a conventional picture of a person choosing stocks, and a reader should not treat any single line as a conviction trade.

The date arithmetic, from the public record: the trades run July 1 to July 31, the report was signed September 8 (39 to 69 days later) and received by OGE September 14 (45 to 75 days later). Any trade dated before July 25 was more than 45 days old at signing. Open Cabinet's own flag marks 260 of the 1,156 lines as filed more than 30 days after notification, a different test from the 45-day trade-date window; this page repeats that count as Open Cabinet's, not as a finding.

Apple: still only Fields buying. Fields's September 8 buy comes against Sen. Pete Sessions's September 22 sale. A second tracked member would promote it to a formal signal; none has traded it.

Intel divergence: unchanged. Pelosi added in July, Evans has been silent since June 10, and nothing in this window moves either side.

Policy Watch

The Senate rejected H.R. 7008. On September 30 the cloture vote on the Stop Insider Trading Act failed 53–47, short of the 60 votes needed, with all 47 Democrats voting against (Daily Caller, Yahoo News). It passed the House 232–198 on July 22. Per the Daily Caller, it will not advance to a final vote.

The two sides' reasons, as quoted: Majority Leader John Thune said Democrats "can't take yes for an answer," pointing to the voter photo ID language attached to the bill (Yahoo News). Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called the bill full of "loophole after loophole" (Daily Caller) and, per Yahoo, "a permission slip for corruption, not a stock-trading ban." Sen. Josh Hawley said he would like "an across the board ban." The bill exempts the president and vice president, which Yahoo noted against Trump's July filing.

What it means for this tape: members can still buy individual stocks and file 30 to 45 days after the fact. The earlier plan to pivot to advance sale notices if the bill passed is moot for now.

On late filings. Late disclosures are common and frequently clerical: a missed form, a spouse's account, a filing agent's backlog. This page reports the gap between trade and disclosure dates exactly as filed, and attributes any characterization of a filing as a violation to the outlet that made it. Whether a given filing actually breaks the law is for the Ethics Committee to determine, not this page.

The lag caveat, every edition. Disclosures lag trades by up to 45 days. Ranges are as filed. Everything here is a disclosure of a trade that has already happened at a price that has already moved. This is disclosure reporting, not investment advice, and it is not a real-time feed of anyone's positions.
