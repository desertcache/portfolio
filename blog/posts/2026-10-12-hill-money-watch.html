Hill Money Watch: October 12, 2026

A quiet week for the active roster: no new filings from Pelosi, Fields, Evans or Rick Scott, and Trump's August-trades report is not posted yet. The big number is a catch-up the last edition missed: Sen. Jim Justice disclosed a partial sale of the Greenbrier in the over-$50M band. Also: Ro Khanna's 400-line September filing and Rep. Diana Harshbarger's $1.85M–6.75M into KKR and Coatue private-markets funds.

October 12, 2026

Window. New filings published October 3 through October 12, the nine days since the October 3 edition, plus two September filings that edition missed. Today is a federal holiday; the newest filings on the trackers are from Wednesday, October 7.

The Headlines

No new buys from the active roster this week. Pelosi, Fields, Evans and Rick Scott filed nothing new. Fields filed on October 1 (reported last edition) and runs on a roughly three-week lag, so his next filing is more likely late October. Trump's report covering August trades has not been posted yet; the recent monthly rhythm put it around October 8 to 15.

The biggest number of the season is a catch-up. Sen. Jim Justice (R-WV) disclosed a partial sale of Greenbrier Hotel Corporation and Affiliates in the "Over $50,000,000" band, the top band on the Senate form. It is dated August 14, the day a $500 million deal gave Kennedy Lewis Investment Management 51% of the resort, and it was filed September 28, on day 45. The October 3 edition missed it. It is a private company, so there is nothing here to trade.

Ro Khanna (Tier 2) filed about 400 lines on October 7, covering trades from September 1 to October 1. Only one is six figures: a $100K–250K sale of a Citigroup note linked to the S&P 500, continuing the structured-note rotation reported September 23. Also catching up: Rep. Diana Harshbarger (R-TN) put $1.5M–6M into KKR's private-equity fund for individual investors and $350K–750K into Coatue's innovation fund on August 26 (filed October 1, one source).

Justice and the Greenbrier. The trade date matches a deal that closed Friday, August 14: Kennedy Lewis Investment Management took 51% of the resort through a new parent company, Greenbrier TopCo, in a $500 million transaction, and the Justice family kept 49% (Casino.org, August 17). SenatorDB lists the line as "Sale (Partial)." The $500 million is the deal's headline value, not Justice's proceeds; the filing says only that the value was over $50 million. Day 45 is the last day of the STOCK Act window.

Harshbarger and private markets. Harshbarger is one of the heavier House filers. Both purchases are vehicles that sell private-market exposure to individual investors: KKR's evergreen private-equity fund and Coatue's closed-end innovation fund, per their SEC filings. Together the four lines come to $1.85M–6.75M. The same filing carries six municipal lines of $100K–250K each: New York City bonds bought and sold on August 19 (+43d), a North Texas Tollway Authority bond sold August 20 (+42d), two Katy (TX) school district lots bought September 2 (+29d) and a Hawaii city-and-county bond sold September 4 (+27d). The whole filing rests on congressstock.com alone; neither SentiSense's nor MarketBeat's profile showed it.

Cross-Signals

Fields and Khanna in September: overlap without a signal. Khanna's dependent-child account sold Apple and Alphabet (GOOGL) on September 4. Fields bought Apple on September 8 and Alphabet (GOOG) on September 14. By this page's mechanical test, that is two divergences between tracked members. On Microsoft they were on the same side (Khanna's dependent on September 4, Fields in two lots on September 10). On Netflix, Khanna's household was on both sides (bought September 4, sold September 25) against Fields's September 14 buy. Don't promote any of it. Khanna's managed accounts traded about 400 lines in a month and bought and sold the same names inside it (Netflix, Nvidia, Amazon), so his side is rebalancing, not a view. Fields remains the only directional buyer in these names.

Intel: a third tracked name, on Pelosi's side. Khanna's spouse bought Intel ($1K–15K) on September 4, 42 days after Pelosi's July 24 add. At that lot size, and from managed accounts, it is a footnote. The open question is still Evans, who sold Intel on May 7 and June 10 and has filed nothing since.

Bloom Energy and GE HealthCare: echoes, not signals. Khanna's dependent bought Bloom on September 25, 59 days after Pelosi's large July add, which falls outside the 30-day window. The GE HealthCare buy is Khanna again, the same member whose August buy reversed the March Davidson and Sewell exits; it stays dormant unless a third member trades it.

Policy Watch

Federal: nothing new confirmed since the September 30 cloture failure on H.R. 7008, which the last edition covered (53–47, short of 60). 247 Wall St.'s October 1 recap adds no later action.

Florida's candidate stock declaration. The Washington Sun (Jenna Monnin, October 12) reports that Florida statute 99.021, signed by Gov. Ron DeSantis in April, requires every candidate for federal office to declare on a state form whether they would personally trade stocks if elected. Six of Florida's 55 congressional nominees checked "yes," and at least two of them told the Sun they have no plans to trade individual stocks: the form's wording on trusts and advisers is vague enough that one campaign said it had "zero guidance." The same story reviews sitting Florida members' past filings. It reports that Reps. Scott Franklin and Lois Frankel "have run afoul of the STOCK Act by filing late disclosures," that Reps. Brian Mast, Debbie Wasserman Schultz, Jared Moskowitz, Kathy Castor and Gus Bilirakis "have each violated the STOCK Act by failing to disclose personal financial trades on time," and that Reps. Salazar, John Rutherford, Greg Steube and Laurel Lee "have also filed late disclosures." It gives no trade counts or dates for these. Franklin's office said a professional adviser has managed his investments since before he took office; Frankel's said she does not personally trade individual stocks; Wasserman Schultz said she is "not involved in the market decisions that the broker makes independently." Salazar's newest filing (October 7) falls inside the 45-day window, at 13 to 35 days.

Data Notes

Corrections to the October 3 edition. It missed the Justice and Harshbarger filings above, both of which landed inside its window (September 28 and October 1). It also called Pete Sessions "Sen."; he is a U.S. Representative (R-TX). That post now carries a dated correction.

Sourcing. Justice's sale appears on both congressstock.com and SenatorDB, and the deal date comes from Casino.org. Harshbarger's lines come from congressstock.com only. Khanna's line count comes from adding up rows across a dozen pages of congressstock.com's feed, so it is approximate. His six-figure line appears on congressstock.com only; AltIndex's view of his recent trades shows nothing above $50K.

Trump timing. Recent 278-Ts arrived about a month apart, each covering trades five to ten weeks earlier. For an August 31 trade, the 45-day outer limit falls on October 15. This page does not treat a filing as late until there is one to date.

Not reported as fact. One aggregator says a standalone version of Sen. Jeff Merkley's stock-ban bill was also blocked on October 1 and that the Senate has recessed until after the November 3 elections. Neither Merkley's Senate site nor wire coverage confirmed either claim this pass.

Standing caveats: dollar figures are disclosed ranges, never exact amounts, and totals here add up the ends of the bands, so they are ranges too. Spouse and dependent transactions are attributed to the member. Trackers routinely disagree on lot counts and filing dates.

On late filings. Late disclosures are common and frequently clerical: a missed form, a spouse's account, a filing agent's backlog. This page reports the gap between trade and disclosure dates exactly as filed, and attributes any characterization of a filing as a violation to the outlet that made it. Whether a given filing actually breaks the law is for the Ethics Committee to determine, not this page.

The lag caveat, every edition. Disclosures lag trades by up to 45 days. Ranges are as filed. Everything here is a disclosure of a trade that has already happened at a price that has already moved. This is disclosure reporting, not investment advice, and it is not a real-time feed of anyone's positions.
